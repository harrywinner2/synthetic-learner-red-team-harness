# Research Notes

What I read before building, and how each piece changed the design. The brief requires
personal research; this is the part of it that left fingerprints on the code.

## Misconceptions in early algebra
- **The reversal error** (Clement, Lochhead & Monk, 1981 — "Students and Professors"). Asked to
  write "there are six times as many students as professors," a large fraction of even
  engineering undergrads write `6S = P`. It's not carelessness; it's a stable
  word-order-to-symbol mapping. → seeded as misconception **M2**, and the
  `translate` skill's trap items are built around it.
- **Slope as "the number in front of x."** Students over-apply the `y = mx + b` pattern and
  read a coefficient as the slope even when the equation isn't solved for y. → **M1**, trapped
  by items like `2x + 3y = 12`.
- **Rate vs starting value.** In contextual linear problems students swap the constant rate
  and the intercept ("$3 base + $2/mile"). → **M3**.
- **The equals sign as an operator.** Carried from arithmetic, `=` is read as "compute the
  answer," which breaks multi-step equation solving. (Knuth et al. on equality understanding.)
  → **M4**.
- **"Bigger number = steeper," ignoring sign.** → **M5**, which deliberately *resists* repair
  in the model, so the metrics show a misconception that survives instruction.

How it changed the design: misconceptions are first-class objects with strengths, items
declare which misconception they trap, and the oracle only repairs a misconception when the
tutor explicitly **probes** the reasoning — matching the finding that re-explaining the
correct procedure doesn't dislodge a misconception; surfacing the student's own reasoning does.

## Learning science & assessment
- **Bayesian Knowledge Tracing** (Corbett & Anderson, 1995): latent mastery with learn / slip /
  guess parameters. → the hidden oracle. Slip and guess give realistic noise; the learn-rate is
  modulated by instruction quality and by the learner's persistence.
- **Transfer vs near-transfer.** Local correctness overstates understanding; transfer to novel
  framings is the harder, truer signal. → the pre/post/**transfer** instrument and the
  transfer-minus-post counter-metric that flags teaching-to-the-test.
- **Productive struggle & the expertise-reversal / assistance dilemma.** Too much help
  (answer-giving, over-scaffolding) suppresses learning and breeds dependence; too little
  crushes fragile learners. → the answer-giving and independence counter-metrics, the
  rapport/agency judge, and the Anxious-Quitter persona as the "don't crush them" check.

## Tutoring best practice & known AI-tutor failure modes
- Socratic questioning and hint **laddering** (recall → represent → first step → self-check)
  rather than answer delivery. → exactly the v1 policy.
- LLM tutors are prone to **sycophancy and answer-leakage** — they validate confident wrong
  answers and hand over solutions under mild pressure. → the v0 baseline is written to do
  precisely this, and the answer-leakage detector + Confident-Guesser persona are aimed at it.
- "I get it" as a **disengagement move**, not a truth claim. → the gate-understanding policy
  (require an unaided solve) and the False-"I-get-it" persona.

## Student motivation & avoidance
Achievement-goal theory (mastery vs performance orientation) and the everyday taxonomy of
avoidance — answer-seeking, stalling, topic-switching, hint-farming, gaming-the-system
(Baker et al. on "gaming the system" in intelligent tutors). → the trait vector
(`gaming_tendency`, `hint_seeking`, `honesty`, `distractibility`) and the five operational
avoidance categories the classifier labels.

## What the research told me I'd get wrong
Synthetic learners generated by one model risk a **monoculture** — they may be too coherent and
share the base model's blind spots, so they can miss failure modes a messy human would expose.
That warning is why the strongest claims in this project are comparative and counter-metric-guarded,
and why the Limitations memo leads with a call for a human-subjects pilot.
