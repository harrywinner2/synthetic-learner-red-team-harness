# Limitations Memo

Read this before believing any number in the product. The harness is designed to produce
*evidence*, and evidence has edges.

## The honest shape of our central claim
We do **not** claim "v1 is a better tutor." We claim: *given these synthetic learners and this
instrument, v1 beats v0 on learning, transfer, misconception repair and answer-discipline,
the counter-metrics that would expose gaming stayed healthy, an independent judge mostly
agreed, and the held-out gap is small and disclosed.* That is a comparative, guarded claim.
The leap to "better for real students" requires human data we don't have.

## What the harness cannot see
- **Non-textual signals.** Real avoidance shows up in latency, affect, body language, the
  half-erased work. We have only text, so we systematically miss whatever isn't said.
- **Long-horizon retention.** We model one session plus an immediate transfer test. We say
  nothing about whether learning survives a week, which is where a lot of real tutoring value
  (and a lot of real failure) lives.
- **Curriculum-scale and sequencing effects.** One topic, five skills. Ordering effects across a
  full course are out of scope.

## Where the synthetic learners themselves are suspect
- **Monoculture risk.** The learners are generated by one model and may be *too coherent* —
  more internally consistent than real teenagers, and blind in the same places the base model
  is blind. A failure mode the base model can't imagine is a failure mode our learners won't
  exhibit.
- **Behaviour is partly scripted.** Trait-driven action selection buys reproducibility at the
  cost of some surprise. A real student's avoidance is more creative than five categories.
- **Mastery is a model, not a mind.** BKT is a useful fiction. The "true mastery" the whole
  system measures against is itself an approximation; calibrating it against human data is
  unfinished work.

## Where the measurement can deceive us
- **Judge reward-hacking.** The avoidance classifier, answer-leakage detector, and evaluators
  are LLMs. A tutor (or a future auto-improver) could in principle learn to satisfy the judges
  without teaching. The independent-judge agreement mitigates this; it does not eliminate it,
  and the two judges may share blind spots.
- **The independent judge is a proxy, not a human study.** "78% agreement" means a second
  model agreed, not that an educator did. We label it as such everywhere it appears.
- **Overfitting to the harness.** We hold out two personas and a problem set to catch it, but if
  the *whole improvement methodology* is tuned to this harness, the held-out split won't reveal
  it. The only real defence is changing the learners and the subject — which we have not yet done.

## Things we chose not to do (and would, next)
- A **human-subjects pilot**: have educators rate a sample of sessions and real students take
  the same instrument, then report harness-vs-human agreement. This is the single most
  important next step.
- A **second subject and a second base model** for the learners, to test whether the
  improvement generalises or was an Algebra-and-gpt-4o-mini artifact.
- **Adversarial robustness** work: the Adversarial Bypasser remains the weakest case; the
  current redirection policy holds the line inconsistently under jailbreak-style pressure.

## One-line version
Treat every metric here as "true within a simulation we built and are actively trying to
disprove." That posture — not the scores — is the deliverable.
